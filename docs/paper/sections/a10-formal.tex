\section{The formal verification}\label{app:formal}

The main theorems are formalized in Lean~4~\cite{Lean4} with Mathlib~\cite{Mathlib20}, in the
repository~\cite{Companion}. This appendix says what the formalization states, how it is checked,
and on what it depends, and gives a dictionary between the paper and the Lean declarations.

\subsection{The statements}\label{app:formal-statements}

The file \texttt{Palomar/Challenge.lean} imports only Mathlib and states the three parts of
\cref{thm:main} as the theorems \lean{octagon_density}, \lean{reinhardt} and
\lean{reinhardt_equality} of the namespace \lean{ReinhardtChallenge}. Its definitions are those of
\cref{def:packing-density}, which is Definition~2.1.1 of~\cite{HalesVajjha2024}. Points of the
plane are maps $\{0,1\}\to\R$ and areas are Lebesgue measure. A convex disk is a compact convex set
with nonempty interior, and a congruent copy of $K$ is its image under a map $x\mapsto Ax+b$ with
$A$ orthogonal. A packing is a family of convex disks whose members have pairwise disjoint
interiors. Its density exists when the upper and lower limits, as $r\to\infty$, of the fraction of
the disk of radius $r$ about the origin that it covers are finite and equal, and $\delta(K)$ is the
supremum of the densities that exist over the packings by congruent copies of $K$. A disk is
centrally symmetric when there is a point $c$ with $2c-x\in K$ for every $x\in K$. The smoothed
octagon is given in Reinhardt's description (\cref{def:smoothed-octagon}): with $d_k(x)$ the signed
distance from $x$ to the line of the $k$-th side of the regular octagon of inradius~$1$, it is the
octagon $d_k\ge0$ with the corner between sides $k$ and $k+1$ cut off along the hyperbola
$d_{k-1}d_{k+2}=\sqrt2/4$. These definitions are copied word for word from the library, so the
theorems the library proves are the theorems the file states.

\subsection{The check}\label{app:formal-check}

The root module \texttt{SmoothedOctagon.lean} restates the three theorems and proves them with no
hypothesis. Lean's kernel checks every step, and every theorem depends only on Lean's standard
axioms \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice} and \lean{Quot.sound}; an audit script checks this
for every declaration of the library. The Comparator configuration
\texttt{Palomar/comparator.json} checks that the root module proves the statements of
\texttt{Palomar/Challenge.lean} with the same definitions. The proof is registered in the Palomar
registry~\cite{Palomar} as \texttt{PALOMAR-2026-11-09-000008}, at trust level high: Palomar's
verifier checked it with the independent kernels nanoda and con-ron at commit
\texttt{9ba6bf8} of~\cite{Companion}. The current library retains these three statements; the names below are those of
its current declarations. Lean and Mathlib are
pinned at version \texttt{v4.36.0-rc2}. The library has about 117{,}000 lines in 378 modules, and a
full build takes about 3.5 CPU-hours.

The library depends on two others. The first~\cite{SmoothedPolygonLean} formalizes the parts of
the book of Hales and Vajjha~\cite{HalesVajjha2024} that the proof uses, together with the classical
results behind them: among them Fejes T\'oth's theorem (\cref{thm:fejes-toth}), the theorems of
Minkowski and Mahler on critical lattices and circumscribed hexagons, the compact domain
(\cref{thm:compact-domain}) and the lift of a global minimizer to a Pontryagin extremal
(\cref{thm:pmp-lift}). The second~\cite{OptimalControlLean} proves the Pontryagin maximum
principle~\cite{Pontryagin1962} with free terminal time, an integral cost and mixed boundary
conditions, by the argument of Agrachev and Sachkov~\cite{AgrachevSachkov2004}, and Filippov's
theorem~\cite{Filippov1962}; Brouwer's fixed-point theorem comes from~\cite{FixedPointLean}.

\newpage\noindent\textbf{Complete and committed (18).}
These groups are integrated into the paper and Lean. Each has passed the full
Lean build, standard-axiom audit, paper check and independent source review.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=2em,itemsep=5pt]
\item[1.] \emph{Symbolic reduction.}
Symbolic bounds on an arbitrary compact region prove the clearance and budget
estimates, with the equilibrium, projective and finite-switch arguments.

\item[2.] \emph{Octagon cost and spectral caps.}
Positive logarithm and inverse-sine remainders prove the cost comparisons.
The golden-unit threshold gives $\gamma^2<5/27$, and the spectral inequality
gives the exact cap $\Gamma_{\rm oct}$. The cover comparisons use the same
moments. The final S6 score comparison remains in group~24.

\item[3.] \emph{M1 symmetric angle gap.}
The constrained root, radial monotonicity and symmetric minimum prove the
angle gap at $c=3/5$. Both physical callers use $\gamma^2\le5/27$.

\item[4.] \emph{M1 two-centre product.}
The exact centre-product bound gives a period cost greater than
$\sqrt3(2\sqrt2-1)$. A strict reciprocal midpoint bound for $\log2$
compares this cost directly with the octagon cost.

\item[5.] \emph{Terminal five-piece comparison.}
An integral kernel proves strict concavity of $h(\sqrt x)$ and the circle
bound, with equality only at equal coordinates. The reciprocal midpoint
bound for $\log2$ proves the strict density gap on the full five-piece domain.

\item[7.] \emph{Forward symmetric neighbour value.}
The endpoint value follows from the analytic boundary comparison.

\item[8.] \emph{Forward symmetric neighbour slope.}
The partial-slope estimate, tangent endpoint and global comparison hold for $h\ge h_0$.

\item[11.] \emph{Mixed norm ceilings and duration.}
A weighted reciprocal mean gives both incoming and central bridge-norm
ceilings through exact positive gaps. Uniform Jensen gives the duration
bound $3d<7c$.

\item[13.] \emph{Smaller-short reverse-centre scale.}
The comparison holds on the full domain $\rho<1/4$.

\item[14.] \emph{Smaller-short bridge norm and endpoint gaps.}
The proof gives the global cap $3/2-\log2$, the tilted-gap comparison and both
exact endpoint gap bounds.

\item[15.] \emph{Smaller-short bridge upper orders.}
Analytic contact comparisons give the bound $5/4$ and, for $x\le5$, the bound $9/8$.

\item[16.] \emph{Smaller-short small-box upper order.}
The proof uses the natural tilted moments and their monotonicity.

\item[17.] \emph{Smaller-short strong lower order.}
Weight-scale comparison, a strict Simpson bound and positive-product identities
prove the estimate for every $x>1$.

\item[18.] \emph{Smaller-short quadratic order.}
Positive contact gains control the curving cost and threshold variation through
a positive normalized budget.

\item[19.] \emph{S4 norm comparison.}
The two central floor tests decrease with the norm for every $u\ge3/4$.
The remaining S4 shape signs are deferred in group~38.

\item[20.] \emph{S5 norm ceiling, duration and shear.}
The global norm ceiling and exact duration tangent reduce the comparison
to two shape endpoints. Resolvent derivatives, face tangents and convexity
prove their signs and supply the continuation bounds directly.

\item[21.] \emph{S6 score continuation.}
Three explicit loss bounds and a natural central-shear bound prove strict decrease
along the exact tangent curve. The physical continuation and completion theorems
accept $-hR_x>2\nu$.

\item[22.] \emph{S6 additional angle and shape concavity.}
An arctangent remainder gives the exact central-score bound. Strict concavity
in $T$ on $27R/31\le T\le R$ reduces the shape comparison to two endpoints,
including zero displacement. Groups~23--24 cover the remaining norm floor,
shear envelopes and final endpoint comparisons.
\end{enumerate}
